China has spent more than two decades building financial and commercial relations in Argentina. From 2000 to 2023, China provided around US$36 billion through 127 loans, 84 grant-financed commitments, and currency swaps to Argentina.

Milei wants a closer relationship with Washington, but does not want to sacrifice his important economic relationship with China. Argentina agreed to prioritize the U.S. as a trading partner for copper, lithium, and other critical minerals, while still keeping Chinese investment open.

The rivalry between Beijing and Washington became visible when the U.S. challenged Huawei’s proposed role in the Vaca Muerta region of Neuquen. Huawei was in negotiations with CALF, the Neuquen electricity cooperative, to provide communications equipment for its operation in the region.

China strongly criticized the move, accusing the U.S. of interfering with commercial cooperation and undermining free-market principles. Beijing argued that Washington used security concerns as a justification to limit Chinese competition in Argentina.

The U.S.-China rivalry is also affecting scientific operations in Argentina. The China-Argentina Radio Telescope (CART) project in San Juan came to a halt after its governing agreement expired. Some scientists and officials are attributing the delay to geopolitical tensions with the U.S.

The competition is even extending to basic infrastructure projects. The Paraná River system is crucial to exports from Argentina and its neighbors. Therefore, an important dredging contract received scrutiny from Washington over concerns about Chinese involvement in the strategic waterway.

Argentina appears willing to move closer to Washington, but does not want to sacrifice its crucial partnership with China. Washington increasingly sees Chinese influence in Argentina as a security concern, while Argentina sees it as an important economic and commercial relationship.

The U.S. has valid concerns about Chinese involvement in infrastructure, supply chains, and communications. But if Washington wants Argentina to rely less on China, they must present an economically viable alternative.

Influence in Argentina is being fought through technology, science, infrastructure, and investments. Ultimately, the battle will come down to which country can deliver Argentina the most economic growth - the United States or China?
